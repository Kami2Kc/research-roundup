The Research Roundup

New economics research for Global Markets: rates, FX, credit, macro and emerging markets.

Editor's note

This issue looks at how shocks travel through global markets. Three papers examine the United States as a source: Treasury bond supply, monetary policy and the global financial cycle, and the safe-haven status of government bonds. Two papers turn to geopolitics, separating feared from realised energy disruptions and tracing how euro area sovereign bond holders react to geopolitical risk. A further group covers market structure and policy design, with evidence on offshore forwards in emerging-market currencies, Bank of Japan equity purchases and stablecoin flows. Taken together, the papers show that the dollar, investor composition and expectations often matter as much as the initial shock.

Transversal  IMF Working Papers · 24 Oct 2026

U.S. Treasury supply shocks lift foreign yields and depress global output

Global Spillovers from U.S. Bond Supply Shocks — Danila Smirnov

The paper asks how changes in the supply of U.S. Treasury bonds spill over to other economies. The author identifies supply shocks from yield moves around Treasury auction announcements and uses them as instruments in daily and monthly local projections covering advanced and emerging economies. Pass-through to foreign long-term rates is substantial: a debt expansion shock that lifts the U.S. 10-year yield by 10 basis points raises foreign 10-year yields by roughly 7 basis points within a few business days. Over eight months, industrial production falls by up to 0.4 percent in the United States and by a comparable amount abroad, with a more persistent profile. The foreign decline reflects financial tightening, exchange-rate depreciation and weaker U.S. demand, and it is larger where foreign investor participation is higher.

Key takeaways

  • A U.S. debt expansion shock that raises the 10-year Treasury yield by 10 basis points lifts foreign 10-year yields by roughly 7 basis points within a few business days.
  • Industrial production falls by up to 0.4 percent in the U.S. after eight months and by a comparable amount abroad, with a more persistent foreign profile.
  • Moving from the 25th to the 75th percentile of foreign investor participation deepens the foreign industrial production decline by 0.5 percentage points.
Why it matters for markets. The results suggest that Treasury issuance and auction outcomes are a driver of global long-term yields, not only of U.S. rates. For rates and FX views, supply news from Washington may feed into foreign curves and currencies, especially where foreign investors hold a large share of the debt. The estimates come from one identification approach, so they are best treated as an indication of scale rather than a fixed rule.

Market focus: Transversal. The paper estimates spillovers from U.S. bond supply to both advanced and emerging economies, so it speaks to both groups.

EM  Bank for International Settlements · 16 Oct 2026

Offshore NDF order flow moves onshore spot rates in emerging markets

Offshore forwards and exchange rate transmission in emerging markets — Gerardo Ferrara, Andreas Schrimpf, Ganesh Viswanath-Natraj, Zijie Wang

The paper asks how offshore non-deliverable forward (NDF) markets pass exchange-rate pressures through to domestic currency markets in emerging economies. The authors use transaction-level data covering the universe of centrally cleared NDFs for ten emerging-market currencies between 2013 and 2025. Their central finding is that offshore NDF order flow moves both offshore forward rates and domestic spot exchange rates. The same order flow also widens the spread between offshore and onshore forward rates. In other words, trading in offshore forwards is not isolated from the onshore market and affects the exchange rates that domestic markets see. The abstract does not report the size of these effects or how they vary across currencies, so the detail is left to the full paper.

Key takeaways

  • The study uses transaction-level data on the universe of centrally cleared NDFs for ten emerging-market currencies over 2013 to 2025.
  • Offshore NDF order flow moves both offshore forward rates and domestic spot exchange rates.
  • Offshore NDF order flow also widens the offshore-onshore forward spread.
Why it matters for markets. The findings indicate that positioning in offshore forwards can feed into onshore spot rates, so NDF flows are worth watching alongside conventional spot indicators in emerging-market FX. A widening offshore-onshore spread may also point to pressure that spot rates have not yet fully shown. The abstract gives no magnitudes, so the practical size of the effect should be checked in the full paper.

Market focus: EM. The paper studies ten emerging-market currencies and how offshore NDF trading affects their domestic exchange rates.

Transversal  NBER Working Papers · 20 Oct 2026

A model linking U.S. monetary policy to global risk premia through dollar funding

A Model of U.S. Monetary Policy and the Global Financial Cycle — Rohan Kekre, Moritz Lenel

The paper proposes a general equilibrium model of how U.S. monetary policy shapes the global financial cycle. Its key agents are arbitrageurs with currency-mismatched balance sheets. If their portfolios are mean-variance efficient, they must be short the dollar. A U.S. tightening therefore reduces their net worth and raises the global price of risk. The authors discipline this mechanism so that it can rationalise the observed effects of U.S. monetary policy on international asset prices. They then quantify the associated macroeconomic spillovers and the role of policy responses abroad. They also consider a potential future with higher dollar interest rates, in which arbitrageurs' dollar funding and the spillovers from U.S. monetary policy are dampened.

Key takeaways

  • Mean-variance efficient arbitrageurs must be short the dollar, so a U.S. tightening reduces their net worth.
  • Lower arbitrageur net worth raises the global price of risk, which transmits U.S. policy to international asset prices.
  • In a future with higher dollar interest rates, arbitrageurs' dollar funding and the policy spillovers are dampened.
Why it matters for markets. The model offers a balance-sheet explanation for why Fed tightening can raise risk premia worldwide, which is useful when thinking about FX and credit reactions to Fed repricing. Its suggestion that spillovers may weaken if dollar rates stay higher is a conditional result from a model rather than a forecast. It also gives a framework for judging how much local policy responses can offset external pressure.

Market focus: Transversal. It is a global general equilibrium model with spillovers to international asset prices across countries, not tied to one economy or group.

DM  Federal Reserve Board · 16 Oct 2026

Did government bonds lose their safe-haven role during the Middle East escalation?

FEDS Note: Government Bonds’ Safe-Haven Property and Inflationary Pressures

This FEDS Note starts from an observation made earlier this year. Escalation of the conflict in the Middle East led to sharp falls in major stock indexes as energy prices rose and the global economic outlook worsened. Prices of long-term government bonds in major advanced economies also fell. That prompted some commentators to question whether government bonds can still be relied on to rally in times of heightened risk aversion and serve as a safe haven. The authors, Canlin Li and Emre Yoldas, use this episode to examine the question. The abstract available here only sets out the motivation and the debate, so it does not describe the methods or the conclusions. Readers should consult the note itself for the analysis.

Key takeaways

  • Stock indexes fell sharply during the Middle East escalation as energy prices rose and the global outlook deteriorated.
  • Prices of long-term government bonds in major advanced economies also declined over the same period.
  • The episode led some commentators to question whether such bonds still act as a safe haven in times of higher risk aversion.
Why it matters for markets. Whether sovereign bonds hedge equity risk in an energy-driven shock matters for portfolio construction and for duration views in developed markets. The episode points to inflationary pressure as a possible reason why bonds and equities fell together, in line with the note's focus on inflation. Since the abstract does not state the note's findings, any conclusion should wait until the full text has been read.

Market focus: DM. The note looks at long-term government bonds in the United States and other major advanced economies.

DM  NBER Working Papers · 14 Oct 2026

Bank of Japan stock purchases: effects depend on yield curve control

The Impact of Central Bank Stock Purchases: Evidence from Discontinuities in Policy Rules — Masao Fukui, Masayuki Yagasaki

The paper measures the effects of central bank equity purchases by exploiting a feature of the Bank of Japan's policy rule, which triggers purchases when movements in a stock market index fall below a threshold. This discontinuity allows the authors to trace the impact of the purchases. In normal times, the purchases raise the long-term interest rate but have no detectable effect on stock prices. After the introduction of yield curve control, which pegs the long-term yield at 0%, interest rates stop responding and stock prices rise robustly after purchases. The results are inconsistent with the inelastic market hypothesis in its original form, but general equilibrium extensions with endogenous interest rates can explain them.

Key takeaways

  • In normal times, Bank of Japan stock purchases raise the long-term interest rate and leave no detectable impact on stock prices.
  • After yield curve control pegged the long-term yield at 0%, rates stopped responding and stock prices rose robustly following purchases.
  • The findings contradict the original inelastic market hypothesis, but general equilibrium extensions with endogenous interest rates explain them.
Why it matters for markets. The paper shows that the effect of equity purchases depends on what the interest rate is doing, which matters when judging how central bank asset purchases transmit to markets. It is also relevant to how the unwinding of unconventional policy might play out for Japanese rates and equities. The evidence is specific to Japan's policy rule, so it should not be applied to other central banks without care.

Market focus: DM. The paper studies the Bank of Japan's stock purchase programme and its effects on Japanese yields and equity prices.

Transversal  IMF Working Papers · 6 Oct 2026

Fear of energy shortages moves prices before supply disruptions arrive

Fear and Shortages — Patricia Gomez-Gonzalez, Régis Kouassi, Jianhong Liu, Joseph Moussa et al.

The paper builds a new high-frequency structural vector autoregression that separates the drivers of oil and natural gas prices in real time. It distinguishes actual supply disruptions from geopolitical-risk shocks that raise expectations of future shortfalls. Local projections show that the macroeconomic effects differ by shock. Oil supply shocks significantly affect aggregate prices and unemployment, whereas geopolitical supply risk shocks have only short-lived effects on prices and none on unemployment. All gas shocks have persistent effects on the price level but muted effects on unemployment. Historical price decompositions for the Russia-Ukraine war and the 2026 Middle East war show that prices rise on geopolitical risk before disruptions materialise, and fears of further shortages keep adding upward pressure afterwards.

Key takeaways

  • The model separately identifies realised supply disruptions and geopolitical-risk shocks that raise expectations of future shortages.
  • Oil supply shocks significantly affect prices and unemployment, while geopolitical supply risk shocks have only short-lived price effects and no unemployment effect.
  • In both the Russia-Ukraine war and the 2026 Middle East war, energy prices rose on geopolitical risk before disruptions occurred.
Why it matters for markets. For inflation and central bank views, the source of an energy price move matters: a risk-driven spike appears to be less persistent for prices than a realised supply loss. This supports a more cautious reading of headline inflation when energy rises on fear rather than on actual shortages. The paper's own message is that policymakers should distinguish the two, and the same logic applies to those pricing policy rates.

Market focus: Transversal. The paper analyses oil and gas price drivers and their macroeconomic effects without focusing on a single country group.

DM  European Central Bank · 30 Oct 2026

How euro area sovereign bond holders respond to geopolitical risk

The effects of geopolitical risk on sovereign debt holders in the euro area

The paper examines how different types of investors in euro area government bonds respond to geopolitical risk in the issuing country. The authors use confidential, granular data on sovereign bond holdings and distinguish investor categories while accounting for the dynamics of their holdings over time. They combine country-level geopolitical risk indices with local projection methods to estimate how each investor group responds to geopolitical risk shocks. A particular focus is on the high-debt countries Italy, France, Spain and Belgium, where the authors ask whether geopolitical risk could trigger shifts in investor behaviour that pose risks to fiscal sustainability. The abstract describes the approach and the question but does not report the results.

Key takeaways

  • The analysis uses confidential granular data on euro area sovereign bond holdings, split by investor type.
  • Country-specific geopolitical risk indices and local projections are used to estimate how each investor group responds to shocks.
  • The focus is on Italy, France, Spain and Belgium, where shifts in investor behaviour could pose risks to fiscal sustainability.
Why it matters for markets. The question matters for euro area spreads and the stability of the sovereign investor base, since the identity of the marginal holder can shape how geopolitical stress reaches bond markets. The abstract does not state the findings, so the paper's conclusions on which investors adjust, and by how much, cannot be given here. It is worth reading for the dataset and the investor-level view of high-debt countries.

Market focus: DM. The paper studies sovereign bond holders in Italy, France, Spain and Belgium, all advanced euro area economies.

EM  IMF Working Papers · 2 Oct 2026

Stablecoin holdings rise after capital controls and currency crises

International Stablecoin Flows, Capital Controls and Currency Crises — Marco Reuter

The paper builds a country-level panel of estimated international stablecoin flows for 188 countries, totalling $21.4 trillion in gross flows between 2018 and 2025. Activity is concentrated in emerging markets and developing economies, which hold three quarters of stablecoins. The authors then look at what happens around capital controls and currency crises. When countries restrict capital outflows, stablecoin activity rises by roughly 30 percent over the following six months, then plateaus, and does not fall back when the restrictions are removed. When currency crises hit, stablecoin holdings increase by roughly 75 percent within a year, and the new holdings persist. The authors conclude that dollarisation may now also operate through stablecoins.

Key takeaways

  • Estimated gross stablecoin flows across 188 countries total $21.4 trillion between 2018 and 2025, and emerging and developing economies hold three quarters of stablecoins.
  • Restrictions on capital outflows are followed by a rise of roughly 30 percent in stablecoin activity over six months, which does not reverse when restrictions are lifted.
  • Currency crises are followed by a rise of roughly 75 percent in stablecoin holdings within a year, and the new holdings persist.
Why it matters for markets. The results suggest that capital controls may be less effective than assumed, as stablecoins provide a route around them, and that stress episodes can leave a lasting shift towards dollar-linked assets. For emerging-market FX and capital flow analysis, stablecoins may become a channel for outflow pressure that is not captured in conventional balance of payments data. The estimates are of flows, and the abstract does not say how they were constructed.

Market focus: EM. The paper centres on emerging markets and developing economies, where most stablecoins are held, and on their capital controls and currency crises.
