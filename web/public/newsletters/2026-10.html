Editor's note

October's papers share a concern with what makes an asset a safe haven and how US conditions travel abroad. Two studies examine the dollar and the Treasury market, one around the April 2025 tariffs and one on bond supply spillovers, while a model of the global financial cycle offers a balance-sheet explanation for how US monetary policy spreads. A Federal Reserve Bank of Chicago paper prices monetary-policy uncertainty, and an ECB paper uses inflation options to read risks across horizons. The issue closes with two papers on alternative stores of value: stablecoins in emerging markets, and gold under geopolitical stress, the latter with an abstract that sets out the question only.

DM  G10 central bank  ·  Deutsche Bundesbank  ·  30 Oct 2026

Dollar weakness after April 2025 tariffs reflects a US institutional credibility shock

What volatility reveals: Agnostic identification of exchange rate and treasury-market dynamics in inconvenient times

Why did the US dollar weaken against the euro and long-term US Treasury yields rise after the 2 April 2025 tariff announcement, even though markets moved into risk-off mode? The authors use high-frequency asset price data and a heteroskedastic vector autoregression, a model that uses changes in volatility to identify structural shocks without imposing strong assumptions. They isolate a safe haven shock, which lowers equity prices and yields and lifts the dollar, and a US Institutional Credibility shock, which raises long-term yields and weakens the dollar. Historical decompositions show the safe haven shock explains most of the initial equity fall and volatility spike, while the credibility shock dominates the later dollar depreciation and wider term spread. The August 2019 tariff escalation, by contrast, was largely a safe haven episode.

Key takeaways

  • The April 2025 market reaction cannot be explained by a single tariff shock; several distinct financial shocks interacted.
  • The safe haven shock explains most of the equity fall and volatility spike; the credibility shock drives later dollar weakness and the wider term spread.
  • August 2019 was largely a safe haven episode, so April 2025 was not simply a bigger version of it.
Why it matters for markets. For rates and FX strategists, the paper offers a way to separate flight-to-safety flows from a rise in Treasury risk premia. When the dollar and long-term yields move against the usual risk-off pattern, it may signal changing confidence in the safe asset rather than a conventional tariff effect. The evidence rests on two episodes, so it informs EUR/USD and term premium views rather than settling them.

Market focus: DM. The paper analyses the US dollar, US Treasuries and equities, mainly the dollar against the euro, so it concerns advanced-economy markets.

Transversal  International organisation  ·  IMF Working Papers  ·  24 Oct 2026

US bond supply shocks lift global long-term yields and weigh on activity

Global Spillovers from U.S. Bond Supply Shocks
Danila Smirnov

The paper asks how shocks to the supply of US Treasury bonds spill over to other economies. The author identifies the shocks from yield moves around US Treasury auction announcements and uses them as instruments in daily and monthly local projections, a regression method that traces responses over time, for advanced economies and emerging markets. A debt expansion shock that raises the US 10-year yield by 10 basis points lifts foreign 10-year yields by roughly 7 basis points within a few business days. Monthly results show contractionary effects: US industrial production falls by up to 0.4 percent after eight months, and foreign industrial production by a comparable amount, with a more persistent profile. The foreign contraction reflects financial tightening, exchange-rate depreciation and weaker US demand. Effects are larger where foreign investor participation is higher.

Key takeaways

  • A bond supply shock that raises the US 10-year yield by 10 basis points lifts foreign 10-year yields by roughly 7 basis points within days.
  • US industrial production falls up to 0.4 percent after eight months; foreign output falls by a comparable amount with a more persistent profile.
  • Moving from the 25th to the 75th percentile of foreign investor participation deepens the industrial production decline by 0.5 percentage points.
Why it matters for markets. The results link US Treasury issuance to global term premia, so heavy supply could tighten financial conditions abroad as well as at home. For rates, it points to a transmission channel to non-US yield curves; for FX and emerging markets, to depreciation pressure, especially where foreign investor participation is high or domestic fundamentals are weak. Magnitudes depend on the auction-announcement identification and should be read with care.

Market focus: Transversal. The paper examines spillovers from US bond supply to both advanced and emerging economies, so it spans both groups.

Transversal  NBER working paper  ·  NBER Working Papers  ·  20 Oct 2026

US monetary tightening raises global risk premia by eroding arbitrageur wealth

A Model of U.S. Monetary Policy and the Global Financial Cycle
Rohan Kekre, Moritz Lenel

The paper asks why US monetary policy moves risk premia and asset prices around the world. The authors build a general equilibrium model in which currency-mismatched arbitrageurs hold mean-variance efficient portfolios and therefore must be short the dollar. A US tightening revalues their wealth, eroding net worth and raising the global price of risk. The authors discipline the mechanism so that it rationalises the effects of US monetary policy on international asset prices, and they quantify the associated macroeconomic spillovers and the role of policy responses abroad. They also consider a potential future with higher dollar interest rates, in which arbitrageurs' dollar funding and US monetary policy spillovers are dampened.

Key takeaways

  • Mean-variance efficiency implies currency-mismatched arbitrageurs are short the dollar, so a US tightening reduces their net worth.
  • Lower arbitrageur net worth raises the global price of risk, which transmits US policy to international asset prices.
  • With higher dollar interest rates in future, arbitrageurs' dollar funding and US monetary policy spillovers would be dampened.
Why it matters for markets. The model offers a balance-sheet channel for the global financial cycle, linking US tightening to global risk premia through dollar funding. For FX, credit and emerging markets, it suggests spillovers depend on the dollar positions of leveraged investors and on policy responses abroad. The result that higher dollar rates dampen spillovers is a conditional model outcome, not a forecast.

Market focus: Transversal. The model is a cross-country framework about global risk premia and dollar funding, with no single economy as its focus.

DM  G10 central bank  ·  European Central Bank  ·  5 Oct 2026

Euro area inflation risks differ across horizons and carry information on persistence

Understanding inflation: insights from the term structure of inflation risks

The paper asks what the term structure of inflation risks, meaning risks at short, medium and long horizons, reveals about inflation dynamics. Using prices of traded zero-coupon inflation caps and floors, the authors develop a non-parametric method combined with a Student's t-copula to estimate spot and forward risk-neutral densities at daily frequency. Long-horizon risks are identified from liquid instruments alone, without forward-starting contracts. Focusing on the euro area over 2009-2026, they find the term structure carries information on the persistence of inflation shocks and on how far changes in the outlook become embedded at longer horizons. Short- and medium-term risks relate mainly to current inflation, confidence indicators, commodity prices and near-term macroeconomic risks, while long-term risks relate more strongly to monetary and financial conditions.

Key takeaways

  • A non-parametric method with a Student's t-copula recovers long-horizon inflation risk from liquid caps and floors at daily frequency.
  • The term structure of inflation risks informs on how persistent inflation shocks are and how far they become embedded at longer horizons.
  • Short- and medium-term risks track current inflation and commodity prices, while long-term risks are more strongly tied to monetary and financial conditions.
Why it matters for markets. For euro area rates and inflation strategists, the paper supports looking beyond a single maturity when judging whether inflation shocks are becoming embedded. Option-based densities may add information on risks to the inflation outlook alongside swap-based measures, with the caveat that they are risk-neutral and so mix expectations and risk compensation. It is also relevant to debates on the ECB's reaction to inflation shocks.

Market focus: DM. The paper estimates inflation risk densities for the euro area, an advanced economy, from traded inflation caps and floors.

DM  Regional Federal Reserve Bank  ·  Federal Reserve Bank of Chicago  ·  1 Oct 2026

Priced monetary-policy risk explains much of the inflation-indexed bond risk premium

Monetary-Policy Risk and Equilibrium Asset Prices
Thomas B. King, Corey Feldman

The paper asks how uncertainty about the conduct of monetary policy is priced in assets. Nominal yields, real yields, stock returns and implied volatilities all move more on days with Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) communication than on blackout days, and respond significantly to changes in interest-rate uncertainty around announcements. A New Keynesian model with stochastic variation in the policy rule quantitatively matches this evidence. In the model, monetary-policy risk adds to excess returns on equities and nominal bonds and accounts for much of the risk premium on inflation-indexed bonds. Transitory increases lower stock and bond prices and weaken the economy, much like aggregate-demand shocks. The effective lower bound on interest rates shrinks the risk but magnifies its asset-pricing effects.

Key takeaways

  • Asset prices vary more on FOMC communication days than on blackout days and respond to interest-rate uncertainty, consistent with policy risk being priced.
  • In the model, monetary-policy risk contributes to excess returns on equities and nominal bonds, and transitory increases lower stock and bond prices.
  • The effective lower bound reduces the size of monetary-policy risk but magnifies its asset-pricing consequences, consistent with the data.
Why it matters for markets. The paper treats central bank communication as a priced source of risk, relevant to term premia in nominal and real yields and to equity risk premia around FOMC meetings. The authors also use the model to assess the rise in policy risk after the 2022 tightening, and it suggests effects could be larger near the lower bound. Results are specific to US data and the model calibration.

Market focus: DM. The paper studies US monetary policy communication and its effects on US yields, stocks and implied volatilities, so it concerns an advanced economy.

EM  International organisation  ·  IMF Working Papers  ·  2 Oct 2026

Stablecoin use rises after capital outflow restrictions and currency crises

International Stablecoin Flows, Capital Controls and Currency Crises
Marco Reuter

The paper builds a country-level panel of estimated international stablecoin flows covering 188 countries, totalling $21.4 trillion in gross flows between 2018 and 2025. Activity is disproportionately concentrated in emerging markets and developing economies, which hold three quarters of stablecoins. The author then examines how stablecoin activity responds to capital controls and currency crises. When countries restrict capital outflows, stablecoin activity rises by roughly 30 percent over the following six months, then plateaus, and does not fall back when restrictions are removed. When currency crises hit, stablecoin holdings increase by roughly 75 percent within a year, and the new holdings persist. Both findings suggest that dollarisation may now also operate through stablecoins.

Key takeaways

  • Estimated gross stablecoin flows total $21.4 trillion over 2018 to 2025, and emerging markets and developing economies hold three quarters of stablecoins.
  • Restricting capital outflows is followed by roughly 30 percent higher stablecoin activity within six months, which does not reverse when restrictions are lifted.
  • Currency crises are followed by roughly 75 percent higher stablecoin holdings within a year, and the new holdings persist.
Why it matters for markets. For emerging market strategists, stablecoins look like a channel that may weaken capital controls and add to dollarisation pressure in stressed currencies. This matters for reading capital-flow data and for judging how durable crisis-driven demand for dollar assets may be. The flows are estimates constructed by the author, so the results should be read as empirical patterns.

Market focus: EM. Stablecoin activity is concentrated in emerging markets and developing economies, where capital controls and currency crises are the central mechanisms.

Study asks how financial conditions shape gold's response to geopolitical risk

Chasing El Dorado: gold under shifting geopolitical and financial conditions
Rafael Guerra, Gabriel Zelpo

This Bank for International Settlements paper examines the safe-haven role of gold, asking how geopolitical risks affect gold prices and whether the effect depends on financial conditions. It analyses state-dependent effects, meaning that the response of gold returns is allowed to differ with the macro-financial environment. The motivation is that geopolitical risks have often been associated with tighter financial conditions, but recent data show a decoupling between such shocks and more restrictive financial conditions. That raises the question of how gold returns are affected by geopolitical shocks in this setting. The abstract available for this issue sets out the question and framing but does not report the estimated results, so we cannot say here how large or consistent gold's response is.

Key takeaways

  • The analysis conditions gold's reaction to geopolitical risk on the prevailing macro-financial environment rather than assuming a single average response.
  • Geopolitical risks are often associated with tighter financial conditions, but recent data show a decoupling between such shocks and more restrictive conditions.
  • The abstract frames the central question as how gold returns respond to geopolitical shocks given that decoupling; it does not report numerical results.
Why it matters for markets. Gold features in safe-haven narratives alongside Treasuries and the dollar, so conditioning its response on financial conditions is relevant to commodity, rates and FX strategists. Because the abstract reports no findings, implications for hedging or positioning should wait for the full paper. The decoupling of geopolitical shocks from tighter financial conditions is itself worth watching.

Market focus: Transversal. The paper studies the global gold price and its response to geopolitical risk without a specific country focus.
